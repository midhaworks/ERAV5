**India** (भारत
Category: ./Category:Articles\_containing\_Hindi-language\_text
  ([Hindi](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hindi_language "Hindi language")) Bhārat Gaṇarājya), officially the **Republic of India**,[[19]](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/India#cite_note-27) also known as **Hindustān**,[[20]](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/India#cite_note-28) is a country in [South Asia](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Asia "South Asia"). It is the world's [seventh-largest country by area](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_countries_and_dependencies_by_area "List of countries and dependencies by area"). It is also the [most populated country](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_countries_and_dependencies_by_population "List of countries and dependencies by population") in the world.[[21]](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/India#cite_note-29) It has been the [world](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/World "World")'s largest [democracy](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Democracy "Democracy") by number of people since 1947.[[22]](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/India#cite_note-FOOTNOTEMetcalfMetcalf2012327-30)[[23]](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/India#cite_note-stein-arnold-31)[[24]](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/India#cite_note-FOOTNOTEFisher2018184–185-32)

India is a [peninsular](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peninsula "Peninsula") country. It has the [Indian Ocean](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_Ocean "Indian Ocean") to the west, the [Arabian Sea](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arabian_Sea "Arabian Sea") in the southwest, the [Bay of Bengal](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bay_of_Bengal "Bay of Bengal") in the southeast, and the [Himalayas](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Himalayas "Himalayas") up north. It has six neighbours: [Pakistan](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pakistan "Pakistan") in the northwest;[[i]](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/India#cite_note-33) [China](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/China "China"), [Nepal](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nepal "Nepal") and [Bhutan](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bhutan "Bhutan") in the north; [Bangladesh](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bangladesh "Bangladesh") and [Myanmar](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Myanmar "Myanmar") in the east; and [Sri Lanka](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sri_Lanka "Sri Lanka") and the [Maldives](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maldives "Maldives") are nearby to the south. Its [Andaman and Nicobar Islands](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andaman_and_Nicobar_Islands "Andaman and Nicobar Islands") share water borders with [Indonesia](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indonesia "Indonesia"), Myanmar and [Thailand](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thailand "Thailand").

[Humans](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Human "Human") came to the [Indian subcontinent](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_subcontinent "Indian subcontinent") from [Africa](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Africa "Africa") more than 55,000 years ago.[[25]](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/India#cite_note-PetragliaAllchin-34)[[26]](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/India#cite_note-Dyson2018p1-35)[[27]](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/India#cite_note-Fisher2018p23-36) They have lived there for a long time. At first, they lived in the subcontinent as [hunter-gatherers](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hunter-gatherer "Hunter-gatherer"). The Indian subcontinent is the second most [diverse](https://simple.wiktionary.org/wiki/diverse "wikt:diverse") region after Africa.[[28]](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/India#cite_note-Dyson2018-28a-37) Humans began to create [settlements](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Settlement "Settlement") in the subcontinent 9,000 years ago, on the western banks of the [Indus River](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indus_River "Indus River"). The settlements became parts of the [Indus Valley Civilisation](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indus_Valley_Civilisation "Indus Valley Civilisation") in the third millennium [BCE](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Common_Era "Common Era").[[29]](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/India#cite_note-Combined-2-38) By 1200 BCE, [Sanskrit](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sanskrit "Sanskrit"), an [Indo-European language](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indo-European_language "Indo-European language"), [spread](https://simple.wiktionary.org/wiki/spread "wikt:spread") to India from the northwest.[[30]](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/India#cite_note-Lowe2015-39)[[31]](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/India#cite_note-Combined-4-Rigveda-40) The first presence of Sanskrit is found in the hymns (songs of worship) of the *[Rigveda](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rigveda "Rigveda")*. The hymns were spread from one person to another [orally](https://simple.wiktionary.org/wiki/orally "wikt:orally"), not by any book. They show the early forms of [Hinduism](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hinduism "Hinduism").[[32]](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/India#cite_note-Combined-3-41) The [Indo-Aryan languages](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indo-Aryan_languages "Indo-Aryan languages") replaced the [Dravidian languages](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dravidian_languages "Dravidian languages") in the northern and western regions of India.[[33]](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/India#cite_note-Combined-4-42) By 400 BCE, the [caste](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caste "Caste") system was developed within Hinduism.[[34]](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/India#cite_note-Dyson2018-16a-43) [Buddhism](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buddhism "Buddhism") and [Jainism](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jainism "Jainism") were also developed in India at the same time.[[35]](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/India#cite_note-Fisher2018-59-44)

India has been a [federal republic](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federal_republic "Federal republic") since 1950. [Its government](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Government_of_India "Government of India") is a democratic [parliamentary system](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parliamentary_system "Parliamentary system"). It is a [multilingual](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Multilingualism "Multilingualism") (multiple languages) and [multicultural](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Multiculturalism "Multiculturalism") (multiple cultures) society.[[36]](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/India#cite_note-Dyson2018-219-45) The [capital city](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Capital_city "Capital city") of India is [New Delhi](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Delhi "New Delhi"). India has the second largest [military force](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_Armed_Forces "Indian Armed Forces") in the world and is also a [nuclear weapon](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nuclear_weapon "Nuclear weapon") state.[[37]](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/India#cite_note-46) India's economy became the world's fastest growing in the [G20 developing nations](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/G20_developing_nations "G20 developing nations") during 2014, replacing the People's Republic of China.[[38]](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/India#cite_note-India_clocks_7.5%_growth_in_January-March_quarter,_becomes_world's_fastest_growing_economy-47) India's [literacy](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Literacy "Literacy") and wealth are also rising.[[39]](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/India#cite_note-48)

India has the fourth largest economy by nominal [GDP](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/GDP "GDP"), the third largest by [GDP (PPP)](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/GDP_PPP "GDP PPP") and is one of the fastest growing major economy. According to New World Wealth, India is the fifth richest country in the world with a total individual wealth of $12.6 trillion.[[40]](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/India#cite_note-India_on_10_wealthiest_country_list,_takes_5th_spot-49)[[41]](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/India#cite_note-India's_large_population_helps_push_it_to_7th_position_in_wealthiest_nations_list-50) However, it still has many [social](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Social_issue "Social issue") and economic issues, for example [poverty](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Poverty_in_India "Poverty in India"), [pollution](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pollution "Pollution"), [social equality](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Social_equality "Social equality"), [religious extremism](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Religious_persecution "Religious persecution"), [terrorism](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Terrorism "Terrorism") and [corruption](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Corruption "Corruption").[[42]](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/India#cite_note-51) India has reduced [its rate of poverty](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Poverty_in_India "Poverty in India") but its [economic inequality](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economic_inequality "Economic inequality") has increased.[[43]](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/India#cite_note-Dyson2018-216-a-52)

India is a founding member of the [World Trade Organisation](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Trade_Organisation "World Trade Organisation") (WTO), and has signed the [Kyoto Protocol](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kyoto_Protocol "Kyoto Protocol"). It is also a member of the [G20](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/G20 "G20") developing nations. India has its own [space agency](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_Space_Research_Organisation "Indian Space Research Organisation") (ISRO). It has done much [research](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Research "Research") throughout the [Solar System](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Solar_System "Solar System"). It has sent [spacecraft](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Solar_System_probes?action=edit&redlink=1 "List of Solar System probes") to the [Moon](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moon "Moon") and [Mars](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mars "Mars"). [Indian movies](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cinema_of_India "Cinema of India"), [music](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_music "Indian music") and spiritual teachings are becoming more important in global culture.[[44]](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/India#cite_note-MetcalfMetcalf2012-266-53) Sources describe it as a [potential superpower](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Potential_superpower?action=edit&redlink=1 "Potential superpower"), because of its rising economy and increase in global influence. India is a [country with nuclear weapons](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nuclear_weapons_states "Nuclear weapons states"). It also has a high [rank](https://simple.wiktionary.org/wiki/rank "wikt:rank") in [military expenditure](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_countries_by_military_expenditures?action=edit&redlink=1 "List of countries by military expenditures"). It has disputes over [Kashmir](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kashmir "Kashmir") with its neighbours, Pakistan and China, since the middle of the 20th century.[[45]](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/India#cite_note-kashmir-disputes-54)

India has the fourth largest number of spoken languages per country in the world, only behind [Papua New Guinea](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Papua_New_Guinea "Papua New Guinea"), [Indonesia](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indonesia "Indonesia"), and [Nigeria](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nigeria "Nigeria").[[46]](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/India#cite_note-55) Most of Indians follow [Hinduism](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hinduism "Hinduism") at 80%, but people of different religions such as [Buddhism](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buddhism "Buddhism"), [Sikhism](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sikhism "Sikhism") and [Islam](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islam "Islam") also live there.[[47]](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/India#cite_note-56)

## Origin of the name

The *[Oxford English Dictionary](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oxford_English_Dictionary "Oxford English Dictionary")* (third edition - 2009) says that the name "India" comes from the [Classical Latin](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Classical_Latin "Classical Latin") name *India*. It was originally used for the [Indian subcontinent](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_subcontinent "Indian subcontinent") and the areas to its east. Latin took the name from Hellenistic Greek *India* (*Ἰνδία*), from [ancient Greek](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancient_Greek "Ancient Greek") *Indos* (*Ἰνδός*) and then from [Old Persian](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Old_Persian "Old Persian") *Hinduš*. The Old Persian name was used for the eastern province of the [Achaemenid Empire](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Achaemenid_Empire "Achaemenid Empire"). The name has a relation with the [Sanskrit](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sanskrit "Sanskrit") word *sindhu*. It means "river", [especially](https://simple.wiktionary.org/wiki/especially "wikt:especially") the [Indus River](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indus_River "Indus River").[[48]](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/India#cite_note-57)[[49]](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/India#cite_note-FOOTNOTEThieme1970447–450-58) The [ancient Greeks](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancient_Greece "Ancient Greece") called Indians as *Indoi* (*[Ἰνδοί](https://www.loc.gov/standards/iso639-2/php/langcodes_name.php?code_ID=)*), which means "the people of the Indus".[[50]](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/India#cite_note-FOOTNOTEKuiper201086-59)

## History

Main article: [History of India](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_India "History of India")

[![](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/3/36/British_Indian_Empire_1909_Imperial_Gazetteer_of_India.jpg/250px-British_Indian_Empire_1909_Imperial_Gazetteer_of_India.jpg)](File:British_Indian_Empire_1909_Imperial_Gazetteer_of_India.jpg)

The [British Raj](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Raj "British Raj")

### Ancient India

In 300 [BCE](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/BCE "BCE"), a king named [Chandragupta Maurya](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chandragupta_Maurya "Chandragupta Maurya") established the [Maurya Empire](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maurya_Empire "Maurya Empire") and under [Ashoka](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ashoka "Ashoka"), united most of the Indian subcontinent under a centralized state.[[58]](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/India#cite_note-67)

One of the oldest languages in the world, [Tamil](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tamil_language "Tamil language"), developed in South India more than 3000 years ago.[[59]](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/India#cite_note-68)

### Indian Empires & dynasties

The [Gupta Empire](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gupta_Empire "Gupta Empire") managed to unify large parts of the Indian subcontinent.The Gupta period is traditionally considered India's [Golden Age](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Golden_age_(metaphor) "en:Golden age (metaphor)") as there were great developments in culture, religion and mathematics.

There were also many other Indian dynasties (empires) such as the Chalukyas, Cholas, Pallavas, and Pandyas.[[60]](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/India#cite_note-69) Southern India at that time was famous for its science, art, and writing.

The [Cholas](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chola_dynasty "Chola dynasty") of [Thanjavur](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thanjavur "Thanjavur") were pioneers at [war in the seas](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naval_warfare "Naval warfare") and influenced [Malaya](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Malaya "British Malaya"), [Borneo](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Borneo "Borneo"), Cambodia. The influence of [Cholas](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chola_dynasty "Chola dynasty") are still noticeable in [Southeast Asia](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southeast_Asia "Southeast Asia").[[61]](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/India#cite_note-70)

The [Vijayanagara Empire](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vijayanagara_Empire "Vijayanagara Empire") was another significant Indian empire.

In the 16th century India came under [Mughal](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mughal_Empire "Mughal Empire") rule. The Taj Mahal was built during the Mughal period.

As Mughal rule weakened other empires like the [Maratha Empire](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maratha_Empire "Maratha Empire") and [Sikh Empire](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sikh_Empire "Sikh Empire") replaced it.

### British Raj

In the 1600s, India came under European colonization, and by 1856 the British controlled most of India.[[62]](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/India#cite_note-71)

British colonial exploitation resulted in the deaths of millions of Indians due to starvation and famine.[[63]](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/India#cite_note-72) The British also introduced railways and banned widow burning.[[64]](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/India#cite_note-73)

In the early 1900s, millions of people peacefully started to [protest](https://simple.wiktionary.org/wiki/protest "wikt:protest") against British rule. One of the people who led the freedom movement was [Mahatma Gandhi](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mahatma_Gandhi "Mahatma Gandhi"), who only used peaceful tactics, including a way called "[ahimsa](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ahimsa "Ahimsa")", which means "non-violence".[[65]](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/India#cite_note-CONCISE_ENCYCLOPEDIA_3-74)

On 15 August 1947, India peacefully became free and independent from the [British Empire](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Empire "British Empire"). India's constitution came into effect on 26 January 1950. Every year, on this day, Indians celebrate Republic Day. The first official leader (Prime Minister) of India was [Jawaharlal Nehru](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jawaharlal_Nehru "Jawaharlal Nehru").

### After independence

[![](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/e/ee/Jawaharlal_Nehru_with_Sardar_Vallabhbhai_Patel.jpg/250px-Jawaharlal_Nehru_with_Sardar_Vallabhbhai_Patel.jpg)](File:Jawaharlal_Nehru_with_Sardar_Vallabhbhai_Patel.jpg)

Jawaharlal Nehru with Sardar Vallabhbhai Patel, the architects of modern India

After 1947, India had a [socialist](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Socialism "Socialism") [planned economy](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Planned_economy "Planned economy"). It is one of the founding members of the [Non-Aligned Movement](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Non-Aligned_Movement "Non-Aligned Movement") and the United Nations. It has fought many wars since independence from Britain, including wars in 1947-48, 1965, 1971, and 1999 with [Pakistan](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pakistan "Pakistan") and in 1962 with [China](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/People's_Republic_of_China "People's Republic of China"). India also fought a war to capture [Goa](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Goa "Goa"), a Portuguese-built [port](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harbour "Harbour") and a city that was not a part of India until 1961. The Portuguese refused to give it to India, so India used force and defeated the Portuguese.

India’s early general elections saw the Congress Party, led by Jawaharlal Nehru, dominate until his death in 1964. Lal Bahadur Shastri briefly took over, followed by Indira Gandhi, who won elections in 1967 and 1971. After declaring a state of emergency in 1975, public dissatisfaction led to the Congress losing power in 1977, with the Janata Party taking over. However, this government lasted only about two years, with Morarji Desai and Charan Singh serving as prime ministers.[[66]](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/India#cite_note-75)

Congress returned to power in 1980, and after Indira Gandhi’s assassination in 1984, her son Rajiv Gandhi won a landslide election. The party lost again in 1989, with a short-lived National Front coalition government under V.P. Singh and Chandra Shekhar. By 1991, no party secured a majority, but Congress formed a minority government led by P.V. Narasimha Rao.

India performed nuclear tests in 1974 and 1998. It is one of the few countries that have [nuclear bombs](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nuclear_bomb "Nuclear bomb").[[67]](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/India#cite_note-India_is_a_Nuclear_State-76)

Since 1991, India has been one of the fastest-growing economies in the world.[[68]](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/India#cite_note-77)

## Geography

[![](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/9/97/Indiarivers.png/250px-Indiarivers.png)](File:Indiarivers.png)

Rivers of India

India is the seventh biggest country in the world by land. It is the main part of the [Indian subcontinent](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_subcontinent "Indian subcontinent"). The [countries next to India](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Borders_of_the_Republic_of_India "Borders of the Republic of India") are [Pakistan](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pakistan "Pakistan"), [Bangladesh](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bangladesh "Bangladesh"), [Myanmar](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Myanmar "Myanmar"), [China](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/China "China"), [Bhutan](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bhutan "Bhutan") and [Nepal](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nepal "Nepal"). It is also near [Sri Lanka](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sri_Lanka "Sri Lanka") and the [Maldives](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maldives "Maldives"), two [island countries](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Island_country "Island country"). The [Andaman and Nicobar Islands](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andaman_and_Nicobar_Islands "Andaman and Nicobar Islands"), a [union territory](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Union_territory "Union territory") of India, is near [Thailand](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thailand "Thailand"), [Indonesia](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indonesia "Indonesia") and Myanmar.[[69]](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/India#cite_note-78)

### Terrain

There are many mountains in the northern part of India. There is the [Himalayas](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Himalayas "Himalayas"), which have some of the tallest mountains in the world.

There are many rivers in India. The main rivers are the [Ganges](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ganges "Ganges"), the [Brahmaputra](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brahmaputra_River "Brahmaputra River"), the [Yamuna](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yamuna "Yamuna"), the Godavari, the Kaveri, the Narmada, and the Krishna.

### Coastline

India is a [peninsula](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peninsula "Peninsula"), which means that it is surrounded on three sides by water. In the west is the [Arabian Sea](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arabian_Sea "Arabian Sea"), in the south is the Indian Ocean, and in the east is the [Bay of Bengal](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bay_of_Bengal "Bay of Bengal").

India's total [coastline](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coastline "Coastline") is 7,517-kilometre (4,700-mile) long.[[70]](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/India#cite_note-sanilkumar-79) The mainland's coastline is 5,423-kilometre (3,400-mile) long. The [Andaman](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andaman_Islands "Andaman Islands"), [Nicobar](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nicobar_Islands "Nicobar Islands") and [Lakshadweep](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lakshadweep "Lakshadweep") islands have 2,094-kilometre (1,300-mile) long coastlines in total.[[71]](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/India#cite_note-FOOTNOTEKumarPathakPednekarRaju2006-80)

Based on [Indian naval](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_Navy "Indian Navy") [hydrographic](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hydrography "Hydrography") [charts](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chart "Chart"), 43% of the mainland coast are [sandy](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sand "Sand") [beaches](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beach "Beach"), 11% are rocky shores and [cliffs](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cliff "Cliff"), and 46% are [mudflats](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mudflat?action=edit&redlink=1 "Mudflat") or [marshy](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marsh "Marsh") shores.[[71]](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/India#cite_note-FOOTNOTEKumarPathakPednekarRaju2006-80)

### Climates

India has different climates.[[72]](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/India#cite_note-CONCISE_ENCYCLOPEDIA_4-81) In [South India](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_India "South India"), the climate is mainly [tropical](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tropics "Tropics"), which means it can get very hot in summer and cool in winter.[[72]](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/India#cite_note-CONCISE_ENCYCLOPEDIA_4-81) The northern part, though, has a cooler climate, called [subtropical](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Subtropics "Subtropics"). The mountainous regions can be [alpine](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alpine_climate "Alpine climate").[[72]](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/India#cite_note-CONCISE_ENCYCLOPEDIA_4-81) The [Himalayas](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Himalayas "Himalayas"), in the alpine climate region, can get extremely cold.

The Himalayas block cold Central Asian winds from blowing into the [Indian subcontinent](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_subcontinent "Indian subcontinent"). It keeps the most of the subcontinent warmer than most places at same [latitudes](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latitude "Latitude").[[73]](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/India#cite_note-FOOTNOTEPosey1994118-82)[[74]](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/India#cite_note-FOOTNOTEWolpert20034-83)

There is very heavy rainfall along the west coast and in the Eastern Himalayan foothills. The west, though, is drier.

#### Monsoon

Because of some of India's [deserts](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Desert "Desert"), the entire country gets rain for four months of the year. That time (usually around June to September) is called the [monsoon](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monsoon "Monsoon"). It happens because the deserts attract water-filled winds from the Indian Ocean, which give rain when they come into India.

When the monsoon rains come late or not so heavily, [droughts](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Drought "Drought") (when the land dries out because there is less rain) are possible.

## Politics

India is a [parliamentary republic](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parliamentary_republic "Parliamentary republic") with a [multi-party system](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Multi-party_system "Multi-party system").[[75]](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/India#cite_note-FOOTNOTEBurnellCalvert1999125-84) It is the largest democracy in the world by the number of people.[[76]](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/India#cite_note-largestdem1-85) It has six [national parties](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_political_parties_in_India?action=edit&redlink=1 "List of political parties in India"), for example the [Indian National Congress](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_National_Congress "Indian National Congress") (INC) and the [Bharatiya Janata Party](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bharatiya_Janata_Party "Bharatiya Janata Party") (BJP). It also has more than 50 [regional parties](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_political_parties_in_India?action=edit&redlink=1 "List of political parties in India").[[77]](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/India#cite_note-FOOTNOTEElection_Commission_of_India-86) The Congress is known as [centre](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Centrism "Centrism") in Indian [political culture](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Political_culture?action=edit&redlink=1 "Political culture"),[[78]](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/India#cite_note-87) while the BJP is known as [right-wing](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Right-wing_politics "Right-wing politics").[[79]](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/India#cite_note-FOOTNOTEMalikSingh1992318–336-88)[[80]](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/India#cite_note-FOOTNOTEBanerjee20053118-89)[[81]](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/India#cite_note-90) The Congress was the [majority](https://simple.wiktionary.org/wiki/majority "wikt:majority") in the [Parliament](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parliament_of_India "Parliament of India") from 1950 to the end of the 1980s. From the end of the 1980s, the BJP[[82]](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/India#cite_note-FOOTNOTESarkar200784-91) and the powerful regional parties are getting more seats in the Parliament over time. This forced the national parties to create [coalition governments](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coalition_government "Coalition government").[[83]](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/India#cite_note-FOOTNOTEChander2004117-92)

### Government

Main articles: [Government of India](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Government_of_India "Government of India") and [Constitution of India](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constitution_of_India "Constitution of India")

India is ruled under the Constitution of India. It is the country's highest [document](https://simple.wiktionary.org/wiki/document "wikt:document") of law. It came into effect on 26 January 1950.[[84]](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/India#cite_note-FOOTNOTEPylee2003a4-93) Its original form said that India would be a "[sovereign](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sovereignty "Sovereignty"), [democratic](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liberal_democracy "Liberal democracy") [republic](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republic "Republic")". In 1971, the statement was changed to "sovereign, [socialist](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Socialist "Socialist"), [secular](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Secularism "Secularism"), democratic republic".[[85]](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/India#cite_note-FOOTNOTEDutt1998421-94)

India has been said to be a "quasi-[federal](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federation "Federation")" form of government. That means a strong [federal government](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federal_government "Federal government") with weak state governments.[[86]](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/India#cite_note-FOOTNOTEWheare198028-95) The federal government is often called the "union government" or the "central government" but after political, economic and social changes at the end of the 1990s, the government became federal.[[87]](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/India#cite_note-FOOTNOTEEcheverri-Gent200219–20-96)[[88]](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/India#cite_note-FOOTNOTESinha200425-97)

#### Branches of government

The union government is divided into three parts: the [legislature](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Legislature "Legislature") (the one that make laws), the [executive](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Executive_(government) "Executive (government)") (the one that applies laws), and the [judiciary](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Judiciary "Judiciary") (the one that makes sure that the laws are obeyed).[[89]](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/India#cite_note-constitution-98) All three parts are in [New Delhi](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Delhi "New Delhi"), the [capital city](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Capital_city "Capital city") of India.

The legislature of India is called the [Parliament](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parliament_of_India "Parliament of India") (संसद
Category: ./Category:Articles\_containing\_Hindi-language\_text
*Sansad*). It is divided into two houses: the upper house [Rajya Sabha](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rajya_Sabha "Rajya Sabha") (Council of States); and the lower house [Lok Sabha](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lok_Sabha "Lok Sabha") (House of the People).[[90]](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/India#cite_note-FOOTNOTEGledhill1970127-99) The Rajya Sabha has 245 members. They remain members for six years.[[91]](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/India#cite_note-FOOTNOTESharma2007161-100) Most members are [elected](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Election "Election") indirectly by the legislatures of [state and union territories](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/States_and_union_territories_of_India "States and union territories of India").[[92]](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/India#cite_note-FOOTNOTESharma2007162-101) The Lok Sabha has 545 members. They remain members for five years. They are elected directly by the people's vote.[[93]](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/India#cite_note-FOOTNOTESharma2007143-102)

The executive is made up of the [President](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/President_of_India "President of India"), the [Vice President](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vice_President_of_India "Vice President of India"), the [Prime Minister](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prime_Minister_of_India "Prime Minister of India") and the [Union Council of Ministers](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Union_Council_of_Ministers?action=edit&redlink=1 "Union Council of Ministers"). The President is the [head of state](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Head_of_state "Head of state") of India.[[94]](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/India#cite_note-FOOTNOTESharma200731-103) The presidents are elected by an [electoral college](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electoral_college_(India)?action=edit&redlink=1 "Electoral college (India)") for a period of five years. The electoral college is made up of members of central and state legislatures.[[95]](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/India#cite_note-FOOTNOTESharma2007138-104)[[96]](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/India#cite_note-FOOTNOTEGledhill1970112-105) The Prime Minister is the [head of government](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Head_of_government "Head of government") of India. The President can choose the Prime Minister, who has most of the power.[[92]](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/India#cite_note-FOOTNOTESharma2007162-101) The President has less power than the Prime Minister. The Union Council of Ministers helps the Prime Minister. It is similar to a [cabinet](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cabinet_(government) "Cabinet (government)") in many countries.

The judicial branch is made up of three types of [courts of law](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Court_of_law "Court of law"): the [Supreme Court](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Supreme_Court_of_India "Supreme Court of India"), the 24 [High Courts](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/High_Courts_of_India?action=edit&redlink=1 "High Courts of India") and a number of trial courts.[[97]](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/India#cite_note-FOOTNOTENeuborne2003478-106) The [Chief Justice of India](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chief_Justice_of_India "Chief Justice of India") is the head of the Supreme Court. The members of the court have the power to stop a law being passed by Parliament if they think that the law contradicts (opposes) the Constitution.[[98]](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/India#cite_note-FOOTNOTESripati1998423–424-107) They can make any government action [invalid](https://simple.wiktionary.org/wiki/invalid "wikt:invalid") if it contradicts the Constitution.[[99]](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/India#cite_note-FOOTNOTEPylee2003b314-108)

### Divisions

For [administration](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Administration "Administration") purposes, India has been divided into smaller pieces. Most of these pieces are called [states](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/State "State"); others are called [union territories](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Union_territory "Union territory"). In total, there are twenty-eight states and eight union territories.[[100]](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/India#cite_note-LOC_PROFILE-109)

States and union territories differ in the way they are represented. Most union territories are ruled by administrators (called Lieutenant Governors) sent by the central government. All the states (and the territories of Delhi and Puducherry) [elect](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Election "Election") their local government themselves.

## Military

Main article: [Indian Armed Forces](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_Armed_Forces "Indian Armed Forces")

The Indian Armed Forces is the country's military. It is made up of an [Army](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_Army "Indian Army"), [Navy](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_Navy "Indian Navy") and [Air Force](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_Air_Force "Indian Air Force"). There are other parts like Paramilitary and Strategic Nuclear Command. Its Commander-in-Chief is the President of India, but the Ministry of Defence manages the military.

In 2010, the Indian Armed Forces had 1.32 million active personnel, making it one of the largest militaries in the world.[[101]](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/India#cite_note-110)

The Indian Army is becoming more modern by buying and making new weapons. It is also building defenses against missiles of other countries.[[102]](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/India#cite_note-111) In the years 2018-2022, India imported more arms than any other nation in the world.[[103]](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/India#cite_note-112) Since its independence in 1947, India fought four wars with Pakistan and a war with China.

## National symbols

[![](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/5/55/Emblem_of_India.svg/250px-Emblem_of_India.svg.png)](File:Emblem_of_India.svg)

National emblem of India

### Emblem

The national emblem of India shows four [lions](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lion "Lion") standing back-to-back. The lions symbolize power, [pride](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pride "Pride"), confidence, and [courage](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Courage "Courage"). Only the government can use this emblem, according to the State Emblem of India (Prohibition of Improper Use) Act, 2005.

### Name

The name *India* comes from the Greek word *Indus*. This came from the word *sindhu*, which, over time, turned into Hind, [Hindi](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hindustani_language "Hindustani language"), or [Hindu](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hinduism "Hinduism").

The preferred [endonym](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Endonym "Endonym") (the name given to the country by its own people) is "Bhārat" in [Hindi](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hindi "Hindi") and other Indian languages. This differs with names that outsiders use for the country.

## Border disputes

Different countries disagree about where India's borders lie.[[104]](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/India#cite_note-113) For example, India claims [Jammu and Kashmir](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jammu_and_Kashmir "Jammu and Kashmir") as an Indian state.[[105]](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/India#cite_note-ipbdMOI-114) However, [Pakistan](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pakistan "Pakistan") and [China](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/China "China") do not recognise this area as part of India.[[105]](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/India#cite_note-ipbdMOI-114) Similarly, the Republic of India does not recognise the Pakistani and Chinese parts of Kashmir.[[105]](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/India#cite_note-ipbdMOI-114)

In 1914, [British India](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_India "British India") and [Tibet](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tibet "Tibet") agreed on the [McMahon Line](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/McMahon_Line "McMahon Line"), as part of the [Simla Accord](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Simla_Accord_(1914)?action=edit&redlink=1 "Simla Accord (1914)").[[106]](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/India#cite_note-simlaCFR-115) Indians and Tibetans see this line as the official border. However, in July 1914, China withdrew from the agreement.[[106]](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/India#cite_note-simlaCFR-115) Neither mainland China nor [Taiwan](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taiwan "Taiwan") recognize that [Arunachal Pradesh](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arunachal_Pradesh "Arunachal Pradesh") belongs to India. According to them, it is a part of [South Tibet](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Tibet "South Tibet"), which belongs to China.[[107]](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/India#cite_note-116)[[108]](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/India#cite_note-IPCS-117)

## Economy

Main article: [Economy of India](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economy_of_India "Economy of India")

### Growth

India's economy is among the world's fastest growing. It is the 6th largest in the world with a [nominal GDP](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nominal_GDP "Nominal GDP") of [$4.15 trillion](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dollar "Dollar") ([USD](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_dollar "United States dollar")).[[109]](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/India#cite_note-:0-118) In terms of [PPP](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Purchasing_power_parity "Purchasing power parity"), the economy is 3rd largest (worth US$8.720 trillion).[[109]](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/India#cite_note-:0-118) The growth [rate](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rate_(mathematics) "Rate (mathematics)") is 8.25% for [fiscal year](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fiscal_year "Fiscal year") 2010. However, that is still $3678 (considering PPP) per person per year.

### Sectors

India's economy is based on:

- [Service sector](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Service_sector "Service sector"): 43%
- Industries: 41%
- Information technology: 7%
- Farming: 7%
- Outsourcing: 2%.

India's economy is diverse. Major industries include [automobiles](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Automobile "Automobile"), [cement](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cement "Cement"), [chemicals](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chemical "Chemical"), [consumer electronics](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Consumer_electronics "Consumer electronics"), [food processing](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Food_processing "Food processing"), [machinery](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Machinery "Machinery"), [mining](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mining "Mining"), [petroleum](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Petroleum "Petroleum"), [pharmaceuticals](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pharmaceuticals "Pharmaceuticals"), [steel](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steel "Steel"), transportation equipment, and [textiles](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Textile "Textile").[[110]](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/India#cite_note-CIA-119)

### Poverty

Despite its economic growth, India continues to suffer from [poverty](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Poverty_in_India "Poverty in India"). Over one quarter of the population (27.5%) was living in poverty in 2004–2005.[[111]](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/India#cite_note-120) In 2007-2008, 80.4% of the population lived on less than US $2 a day.[[112]](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/India#cite_note-121) By 2009, this number had decreased to 68%.[[113]](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/India#cite_note-122)

## People

[![](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/0/03/India_population_density_map_en.svg/250px-India_population_density_map_en.svg.png)](File:India_population_density_map_en.svg)

Map of the population density of India

There are 1.4 billion people living in India.[[114]](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/India#cite_note-123) In 2023, India passed China to become the world's most [populous](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Population "Population") country.[[115]](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/India#cite_note-pop-124) About 65% of Indians live in rural areas, or land set aside for farming.[[116]](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/India#cite_note-Census-125) The largest cities in India are [Mumbai](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mumbai "Mumbai"), [Kolkata](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kolkata "Kolkata"), Delhi, [Chennai](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chennai "Chennai"), [Bangalore](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bangalore "Bangalore"), [Hyderabad](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hyderabad,_India "Hyderabad, India"), and [Ahmedabad](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ahmedabad "Ahmedabad").[[100]](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/India#cite_note-LOC_PROFILE-109) [Hindi](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hindi "Hindi") and [English](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/English_language "English language") are official languages of India. India has 23 officially recognised languages.[[117]](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/India#cite_note-languages_of_India-126) Altogether, 1,625 languages are spoken in India.[[118]](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/India#cite_note-Manorama-127)

## Languages

### Language families

There are many different languages and cultures in India.[[100]](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/India#cite_note-LOC_PROFILE-109) There are two main language families in India, the [Indo-Aryan](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indo-Aryan_languages "Indo-Aryan languages") and the [Dravidian languages](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dravidian_languages "Dravidian languages"). About 69% of Indians speak an Indo-Arayan language, and about 26% speak a Dravidian language. Other languages spoken in India come from the [Austro-Asiatic](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Austro-Asiatic_languages "Austro-Asiatic languages") group. Around 5% of the people speak a [Tibeto-Burman](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tibeto-Burman_languages "Tibeto-Burman languages") language.

### Hindi & English

[Hindi](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hindi "Hindi") is the official language in India with the largest number of speakers.[[119]](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/India#cite_note-128) It is the official language of the union.[[120]](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/India#cite_note-129) Native speakers of Hindi represented about 41% of the Indian population in the 2001 Indian [census](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Census "Census").

English is also used, mostly for business and in administration. It has the status of a 'subsidiary official language'.[[121]](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/India#cite_note-English-subsidiary-130)

### Other languages

The constitution also recognises 21 other languages - either because many people speak them, or because they are very important for Indian culture. There may be as many as 1,652 different [dialects](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dialect "Dialect") in India.[[118]](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/India#cite_note-Manorama-127)

In the south of India, many people speak [Kannada](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kannada "Kannada"), [Telugu](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Telugu "Telugu"), [Tamil](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tamil_language "Tamil language") and [Malayalam](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malayalam "Malayalam"). In the north, many people speak [Punjabi](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Punjabi_language "Punjabi language"), [Bengali](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bengali_language "Bengali language"), [Gujarati](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gujarati_language "Gujarati language"), and [Marathi](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marathi_language "Marathi language"), [Odia](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Odia "Odia"), and [Maithili](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maithili_language "Maithili language").[[122]](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/India#cite_note-pandit1977-131)[[123]](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/India#cite_note-britannica1993-132)

India has 23 official languages. Its constitution lists the name of the country in each of the languages.[[124]](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/India#cite_note-133) [Hindi](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hindi "Hindi") and English (listed in boldface) are the "official languages of the union" (Union meaning the Federal Government in Delhi);[[125]](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/India#cite_note-134) [Tamil](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tamil_language "Tamil language"), [Sanskrit](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sanskrit "Sanskrit"), [Telugu](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Telugu "Telugu"), [Kannada](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kannada "Kannada"), [Malayalam](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malayalam "Malayalam"), and [Odia](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Odia "Odia") are officially the "[classical languages of India](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Classical_language?action=edit&redlink=1#Classical_languages_of_India "Classical language")."

[Cave paintings](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cave_painting "Cave painting") from the [Stone Age](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stone_Age "Stone Age") are found across India. They show dances and [rituals](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ritual "Ritual") and suggest there was a [prehistoric](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/History "History") religion. During the Epic and [Puranic](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Purana "Purana") periods, the earliest versions of the epic poems *[Ramayana](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ramayana "Ramayana")* and *[Mahabharata](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mahabharata "Mahabharata")* were written from about 500–100 BCE,[[127]](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/India#cite_note-136) although these were [orally](https://simple.wiktionary.org/wiki/oral "wikt:oral") [transmitted](https://simple.wiktionary.org/wiki/transmission "wikt:transmission") for centuries before this period.[[128]](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/India#cite_note-Rine28-137) Other Indian Stone Age sites apart from [Pakistan](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pakistan "Pakistan") are in modern India, such as the [Bhimbetka rock shelters](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bhimbetka_rock_shelters "Bhimbetka rock shelters") in central [Madhya Pradesh](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Madhya_Pradesh "Madhya Pradesh") and the Kupgal petroglyphs of eastern Karnataka, contain rock art showing religious rites and evidence of possible [ritualised](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ritual "Ritual") music.[[129]](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/India#cite_note-138)

[![](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/9/9c/Golden_Temple_India.jpg/250px-Golden_Temple_India.jpg)](File:Golden_Temple_India.jpg)

The Harmandir Sahib or The Golden Temple of the [Sikhs](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sikh "Sikh")

Several modern religions are linked to India,[[130]](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/India#cite_note-EB-139) namely modern [Hinduism](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hinduism "Hinduism"), [Jainism](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jainism "Jainism"), [Buddhism](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buddhism "Buddhism") and [Sikhism](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sikhism "Sikhism"). All of these religions have different *schools* (ways of thinking) and traditions that are related. As a group they are called the [Eastern religions](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eastern_religions "Eastern religions"). The Indian religions are similar to one another in many ways: The basic beliefs, the way [worship](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Worship "Worship") is done and several religious practices are very similar. These similarities mainly come from the fact that these religions have a common history and common origins. They also influenced each other.

The religion of [Hinduism](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hinduism "Hinduism") is the main faith followed by 79.80% of people in the Republic of India; [Islam](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islam "Islam") – 14.23%; Christianity – 2.30%; Sikhism – 1.72%; [Buddhism](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buddhism "Buddhism") – 0.70% and [Jainism](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jainism "Jainism") – 0.37%.[[131]](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/India#cite_note-CensusRel-140)

## Technology

[![](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/b/bd/Indian_Space_Research_Organisation_Logo.svg/250px-Indian_Space_Research_Organisation_Logo.svg.png)](File:Indian_Space_Research_Organisation_Logo.svg)

The logo of ISRO

India sent a spacecraft to [Mars](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mars "Mars") for the first time in 2014. That made it the fourth country and first Asian country to do so, successfully. It was called the [Mars Orbiter Mission](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mars_Orbiter_Mission "Mars Orbiter Mission").[[132]](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/India#cite_note-141)

[ISRO](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_Space_Research_Organization "Indian Space Research Organization") launched 104 satellites in a single mission to create a world record. India became the first nation in the world to have launched over a hundred satellites in one mission. That was more than the 2014 Russian record of 37 satellites in a single launch.

This historic event of Chandrayaan-III[[133]](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/India#cite_note-142) successfully landed on the [South Pole of the moon](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Pole_of_the_moon?action=edit&redlink=1 "South Pole of the moon"), being the first one to do so.

## Pop culture

India has the largest movie industry in the world. The Hindi film industry is known as [Bollywood](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bollywood "Bollywood"), and is mainly based in Bombay, now known as [Mumbai](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bombay "Bombay"). Other industries include [Tollywood](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Telugu_cinema "Telugu cinema"), Kollywood, Sandalwood, Mollywood, Jollywood, Dhollywood, etc. It makes 1,000 movies a year, about twice as many as [Hollywood](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hollywood "Hollywood").[[134]](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/India#cite_note-143)

## Sports

Main article: [Sports in India](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sports_in_India "Sports in India")

[![Cricketers in a game in front of nearly-full stands.](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/6/67/IPL_T20_Chennai_vs_Kolkata.JPG/250px-IPL_T20_Chennai_vs_Kolkata.JPG)](File:IPL_T20_Chennai_vs_Kolkata.JPG)

A 2008 Indian Premier League Twenty20 cricket match being played between the Chennai Super Kings and Kolkata Knight Riders

### Cricket

[Cricket](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cricket "Cricket") is the most popular sport in India. The Indian cricket team won the 1983 and [2011 Cricket World Cup](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/2011_Cricket_World_Cup "2011 Cricket World Cup") and the [2007 ICC World Twenty20](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/2007_ICC_World_Twenty20?action=edit&redlink=1 "2007 ICC World Twenty20"). They [shared](https://simple.wiktionary.org/wiki/share "wikt:share") the 2002 ICC Champions Trophy with [Sri Lanka](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sri_Lanka "Sri Lanka") and won the 2013 ICC Champions Trophy and 2025 ICC Champions Trophy.

Cricket in India is controlled by the Board of Control for Cricket in India or BCCI. Domestic tournaments are the Ranji Trophy, the Duleep Trophy, the Deodhar Trophy, the Irani Trophy, and the Challenger Series. There is also the Indian cricket league and Indian premier league Twenty20 competitions.

### Other sports

Indians have excelled in hockey. They have also won eight gold, one silver, and two bronze medals at the [Olympic Games](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Olympic_games "Olympic games").

Tennis has become popular due to the victories of the India Davis Cup team.

Association football is also a popular sport in northeast India, West Bengal, Goa and Kerala.[[135]](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/India#cite_note-Soccer-144) The Indian national football team has won the South Asian Football Federation Cup many times.

[Chess](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chess "Chess"), which originated in India, is also becoming popular. The number of Indian [Grandmasters](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grandmaster "Grandmaster") has increased recently.[[136]](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/India#cite_note-Anand_crowned_World_champion-145)[[137]](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/India#cite_note-146)

Traditional sports include kabaddi, kho kho, and gilli-danda, which are played throughout India.
